    Results

    The outputs from STCM provide robust computational support for the linguistic alignment proposed by the Two-Source Hypothesis.

    1. Triple Tradition Baseline (Signature A)

    When analysing the 49 Triple Tradition pericopes where Matthew, Mark, and Luke all overlap, the mean cosine similarity between Matthew and Luke is 0.935 (σ = 0.038, 95% CI [0.924, 0.945]). Because we know under Markan priority that both authors drew from Mark, this gives us a quantitative measure of what a "shared source derivation" looks like in the Ancient-Greek-BERT space.

    2. Double Tradition Q-Scoring

    We scored 36 Double Tradition pericopes against this baseline. The mean Q-Score is 0.612 ± 0.057. The top-scoring pericopes represent passages where Matthew and Luke are exceptionally similar — often exceeding the similarity found in the Triple Tradition baseline.

    Top 5 most Q-like pericopes:

    1. Serving Two Masters (Q = 0.710)
    2. Lament over Jerusalem (Q = 0.689)
    3. Return of Unclean Spirit (Q = 0.688)
    4. Jesus on John (Q = 0.679)
    5. Thief in the Night (Q = 0.677)

    Full data for all 36 pericopes is provided in the article (Appendix A, Table A1) and in the CSV output (outputs/reports/q_score_distribution.csv).

    3. Statistical Significance

    Random Null

    To verify these results are not due to general vocabulary overlap in Koine Greek, we ran 1000 permutations where Matthew and Luke pericopes were randomly paired. The observed mean Q-score (0.612) vastly outperformed the null distribution (mean = 0.411, σ = 0.003).

    • Empirical p-value: 0.001 (0 of 1,000 permutations ≥ observed; reported as (b+1)/(N+1))

    (The corresponding z-score of 77.8 reflects the tiny standard deviation of the null mean distribution, not a per-pericope effect size.)

    Thematic Null

    A more demanding test pairs each Matthean pericope with a thematically similar Lukan pericope (e.g., wisdom with wisdom, apocalyptic with apocalyptic). This controls for the possibility that topical overlap alone inflates similarity.

    • Empirical p-value: 0.001 (0 of 1,000 permutations ≥ observed)
    • The correctly paired pericopes still significantly exceed the thematic baseline.

    4. Robustness Analyses

    Weight Sensitivity

    The composite Q-score weights (0.5, 0.3, 0.2) were tested against eight alternative configurations (including equal weights and single-component models). Serving Two Masters ranked first under all nine schemes, and the top-5 set remained highly stable (high Jaccard similarity).

    Sentence-Level Bootstrap

    Resampling sentences within each pericope (200 resamples) yields a mean perturbation standard deviation below 0.05, confirming that Q-scores are robust to variation in sentence composition.

    Word-Overlap Comparison

    The Pearson correlation between embedding-based Q-scores and word-level Jaccard coefficients is high (r = 0.823) but meaningfully below 1.0, demonstrating that the embedding analysis captures semantic and syntactic structure beyond what traditional verbal-agreement statistics measure.

    5. Goulder Redaction Test

    The ten pericopes Goulder (1989) identifies as exhibiting Lukan redaction of Matthew show a lower mean Q-score than the remaining 26 pericopes, but the difference is not statistically significant (Welch's t-test, p = 0.192). However, with group sizes of n = 10 vs. n = 26, the test is severely underpowered, and the medium effect size (Cohen's d = −0.571) indicates a potentially meaningful difference that the current sample cannot confirm or rule out. The result is genuinely inconclusive — it neither supports nor refutes the Farrer Hypothesis. Future work with larger datasets or directional-dependence metrics is needed to resolve this question.

    6. BERT Validation

    Known synoptic parallels produce high cosine similarities; unrelated passage pairs produce substantially lower similarities. The separation confirms that the model is adequately calibrated for NT Greek.

    Conclusion

    The geometry of the Double Tradition in semantic space is highly non-random. Its relationship patterns closely mimic, and in some cases exceed, the mathematical signatures of the known shared-source relationship observed in the Triple Tradition. The signal survives both random and thematic null tests, is insensitive to weight choice, and captures information beyond simple lexical overlap. The Goulder redaction test remains inconclusive due to limited sample size, and readers should note that the Mark centroid baseline, being derived from narrative material, may introduce a degree of structural distortion when applied to predominantly discourse-oriented double-tradition pericopes. Whilst this does not definitively prove a written document called Q, the embedding evidence is strongly inconsistent with random or thematically matched pairing under our null models, and consistent with some form of non-independent literary relationship (shared source and/or direct dependence).
