# Frequently Asked Questions

### Does STCM prove that Q existed?
No. Computational linguistics cannot "prove" the historical existence of a lost document, and STCM does not try to. What it shows is that a contextual representation of Koine Greek passes positive and negative controls drawn from known dependence (Matthew and Luke copying Mark), and that, measured against those controls, Matthew–Luke agreement in the double tradition is closer than two-step Markan copying and at least as close as one-step copying. That is consistent with close written dependence of *some* kind — a shared source or Luke's direct use of Matthew — and the method cannot discriminate between the two.

### Why use Koine-Greek-BERT?
Contextual models represent each word in the light of its passage, so they can in principle recognise when Matthew and Luke say the same thing in different words. Whether they actually do is tested rather than assumed: the model must pass the triple-tradition positive control, and its increment over plain word overlap is measured directly (see the next question). Koine-Greek-BERT is domain-adapted on Koine (LXX, NT, Apostolic Fathers, historians); the central result is replicated with Ancient-Greek-BERT, which was *not* trained on the New Testament.

### Doesn't the statistic just measure word overlap?
Largely, yes — and the project now says so. Word overlap (multiset Jaccard) reproduces most of the ranking (Spearman ρ = 0.87) and the same ordering of the traditions. What the embedding adds is measured in `baselines.py`: after removing the linear effect of word overlap, the residual still separates true parallels from mismatched pairs (*p* < 0.001 against all nulls), and the passages the embedding rates higher than their word overlap predicts are paraphrases (*Conditions of Discipleship*, *One Taken, One Left*, the *Golden Rule*, the *Lord's Prayer*). The increment is modest but real.

### What about the circularity problem — aren't similar topics naturally similar?
This was addressed with the **thematic-null permutation test**. Instead of pairing Matt–Luke pericopes randomly, this test pairs each Matthean pericope with a *thematically similar* Lukan pericope (wisdom with wisdom, apocalyptic with apocalyptic, etc.). The correctly paired pericopes still significantly exceed this thematic baseline (empirical *p* < 0.001, 1,000 permutations), showing that the signal reflects genuine textual correspondence beyond mere topical similarity. A second kind of circularity is length: true parallels are of similar length, random pairs are not, and unrelated passages of different lengths are less similar than unrelated passages of equal length. Two further nulls therefore pair each Matthean pericope with a Lukan one of comparable length (optionally also of the same theme); the signal survives both (*p* < 0.001).

### Are the results sensitive to tuning?
The mean-centred cosine has no weights or free parameters. The analytical choices that remain are tested: the centring vector (six definitions — the double-versus-triple ordering holds in all six; the double-versus-Luke–Mark ordering does not), the layer (last vs mean of last four), the encoder (Koine-Greek-BERT vs Ancient-Greek-BERT) and the encoding of composite units (range-wise vs single sequence; individual pericopes move by up to 0.14, corpus-level contrasts do not change sign). An earlier weighted composite index is retired and survives only as a legacy output.

### What if I don't have internet access to download the model?
STCM includes an offline fallback, but it must be enabled explicitly. If `ABeZet/Koine-Greek-BERT` cannot be loaded via the `transformers` library, the pipeline stops with an error unless `STCM_ALLOW_FALLBACK=1` is set, in which case a deterministic character-trigram hashing embedder (256-dimensional) is used. The fallback is sufficient to demonstrate that the pipeline runs end-to-end, but its results are not comparable to the published outputs; the embedder actually used is recorded in `system_validation.txt`.

### What is the Farrer Hypothesis and does STCM disprove it?
The Farrer Hypothesis argues that Luke used Matthew as a source, meaning there is no "Q". STCM does not directly falsify this model. However, a **Goulder redaction test** compares pericopes that Goulder (1989) identifies as demonstrating Lukan redaction of Matthew against the rest. These pericopes show no statistically significant difference in centred-cosine distribution (Welch's *t* = −1.49, *p* = 0.17), but the test is underpowered (*n* = 10 vs. *n* = 41) and the medium-to-large effect size (Cohen's *d* = −0.75) indicates a potentially meaningful difference that the current sample cannot confirm or rule out. The result is therefore genuinely **inconclusive** — it neither supports nor refutes either hypothesis. A direct directionality test (which evangelist's embeddings predict the other's better, by leave-one-out ridge regression) was also built and controlled: it finds a small asymmetry in the double tradition, but the asymmetry depends on the ridge penalty, vanishes among pairs of equal length, and — decisively — the same statistic does not recover the known direction of copying where Matthew and Luke copy Mark. A method that fails its positive control is not allowed to answer the question, so STCM draws no directional inference.

### What about the minor agreements?
They are visible in the geometry. In the triple tradition, what Matthew does to Mark and what Luke does to Mark are correlated pericope by pericope (0.364 against 0.058 for mismatched pericopes, *p* < 0.001). The geometry cannot tell whether this reflects Luke's knowledge of Matthew or coincident redaction of the same Markan text.

### What about the possibility that Q was in Aramaic?
Casey (2002) argues that Q may have been composed in Aramaic and translated independently into Greek. If so, the Greek-language embeddings would capture two independent translation events rather than the original source. This would *attenuate* the signal (making significant results more noteworthy, not less) and could explain why some pericopes score lower than expected despite being considered core Q material. It is also worth noting that a Koine Greek BERT model may be sensitive to the distinctive syntax of "translation Greek" — Semitisms and Aramaisms that arise when Greek renders Semitic source constructions. If Matthew and Luke translated an Aramaic Q independently, the resulting variance in Greek syntax would naturally lower cosine similarity, which is consistent with the spectrum of centred cosines observed across the double tradition.

### Does passage length or literary form drive the ranking?
Length does not: the correlation between centred cosine and length in characters is essentially zero (*r* = 0.002). Literary form shows a pattern — discourses highest, parables lowest — but it is not statistically significant within the double tradition (Kruskal–Wallis *p* = 0.093). Across traditions, however, the kind of material matters: sayings-dominant triple-tradition units agree more closely than narrative ones, and part of the double tradition's advantage over two-step Markan copying is a sayings-versus-narrative effect (`form_control.py`).

### How were the pericopes delimited?
By the Aland synopsis and the IQP inventory, under four rules enforced by the loader: each pair is trimmed to the verses that are actually parallel (non-contiguous ranges such as Luke 3:7–9 + 16–17 are supported); no verse belongs to two units of the same set; no verse of Matthew or Luke belongs to both the calibration and the test set (Mark–Q overlaps are excluded from calibration); and a calibration unit must be the Markan episode itself in each gospel. The table has 51 double-tradition and 45 triple-tradition units; 16 double-tradition units with a Markan parallel are flagged and the results reported with and without them. The article shows, with *John's Preaching*, how much a wrong boundary can move a single pericope (rank 47 with Luke 3:7–9 alone, rank 20 with 3:7–9 + 16–17).

### Can I run this on John or the non-canonical gospels?
Yes, the pipeline can be adapted to any Greek text, but you will need to map the pericopes in `data_loader.py` (and add their metadata in `stcm/metadata.py`). Currently, it uses a hardcoded table based on the Aland Synopsis and the IQP inventory for the synoptic gospels.
